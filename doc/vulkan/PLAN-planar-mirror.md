# The legacy mirror as a planar reflection (plan, 2026-11-11)

Adam, 2026-11-11: "let's go with planar mirror then and work from there". Why: the hero probe renders six cube faces (one every frame or two, at 686 px for 90 degrees at the default 1024 and the render scale) from the mirrored camera and the glass samples that cube. A mirror seen across a room covers about 8 x 18 degrees of the cube, so the glass magnifies the cube about 3 times: a blurred reflection, with a gloss mip on top, and thin layers (the pink shell over the metal limbs) lost in depth and size. Measured 2026-11-11: raising the resolution to 2048 made the reflection sharp; the glTF blend and lit blend draws are rendered into the faces (test colours in the raw face images), they are only tiny. A flat mirror does not need a cube.

## Design

One extra view of the world per frame, rendered from the **reflected camera**, at the main view's own pixel grid, into a 2D target. The glass is lit by reading that target at the pixel's own screen position.

- **Camera.** The main camera mirrored across the mirror's plane: position and look / up vectors reflected, so the camera stays right handed (no negative scales, no flipped winding); the same field of view and aspect as the main camera. The reflected image comes out mirrored left to right, so the glass reads it at `u = 1 - screen_u`. A glass pixel at screen position (u, v) then shows exactly what the reflected camera sees at (1 - u, v). No parallax, no box, no mip blur by the lookup.
- **Clip.** What lies behind the plane is thrown away by the clip the mirror passes already have (`mirror_clip`, `mirror_flag`, `clipPlane`, working since the environment epoch fix of today: every program takes them). The near plane follows the mirror (the near plane change of today: half the plane distance; an oblique near plane exactly on the mirror is better still, step 6).
- **The view is a secondary view like a cube face.** It reuses `gCubeSnapshot` semantics (no occlusion queries, no RT sun, no DLSS / velocity / SSAO / SSR, `mRT = &mHeroProbeRT`), `cubeSnapshot`'s camera / viewport / render-type handling and `display_cube_face`. What changes: the camera, the viewport (the main render resolution, aspect not 1), and that nothing is copied into a cube array: the face's lit `screen` target IS the reflection.
- **Where it is read.** `probe_hero` (native `lib/probe.glsl`, used by `resolve.frag`, `lib/material.glsl`, `alpha_pbr.frag`) and `tapHeroProbe` (stock `reflectionProbeF.glsl`, used by the soften RT variants and the stock alpha shaders, which RT-on runs use): the same box / sphere weight picks the mirror's own pixels, the cube lookup becomes a 2D lookup at `(1 - u, v)`. A rough surface (gloss below 1) reads a mip of the target (steps 5).
- **When.** After the camera is final: today `LLHeroProbeManager::update / renderProbes` run at the top of `display()` (llviewerdisplay.cpp:899), before `display_update_camera()` (919), so the view would lag the camera by a frame, which a planar view shows (a cube hides it). The mirror view is rendered after the camera update and the geometry updates, before the main cull and render.
- **Cost.** One more view per frame instead of 2 of 6 faces: at the main render resolution it is about the cost of a second main view without RT, DLSS, SSAO (the shadow cascades are the open question: fit to the reflected frustum, 2 like the faces or 4). To be measured at the quiet spot in GPU ms, not fps. Cheaper later: render only the glass's screen rectangle (a sub-frustum), skip when the glass is off screen or occluded, a render scale setting for the target.

## Steps (each one a lap with the screenshot and the glass grab; the faces sheet gives way to a mirror view dump)

1. **The reflected view renders.** `LLViewerWindow::mirrorSnapshot`: camera reflected across the hero probe's plane (`mMirrorPosition`, `mMirrorNormal`), viewport = the main render size, `display_cube_face`, the target stays (a second RT pack, sized like the main one). Diagnostic: dump the target to `run-logs` and show it next to the screenshot (the face dump machinery). The old cube path stays and still draws the glass.
2. **The glass reads it (native path, RT off first).** A new texture slot in the pass state (`PassFastState` id and a native slot `ll_ti_mirror_view`), the sampler uniform in the stock programs; `probe_hero` samples the 2D target at `(1 - u, v)`; weight and fade as today. Lap: RT off, glass shows the reflected view at screen resolution.
3. **Stock shaders (RT on).** The same lookup in `tapHeroProbe` (`reflectionProbeF.glsl`) and its binding (`bindReflectionProbes`, `bindFastUnit` case 10 is the cube today). Lap: RT on with materials (Adam's start), legacy mirror.
4. **Camera timing.** Move the render after `display_update_camera` and the geometry update; check there is no lag when the camera moves (a lap with the camera turning, the glass grab).
5. **Roughness.** Mips of the target for glossy but not perfect surfaces; the cube's mip chain and the radiance programs for the hero probe (`gHeroRadianceGenProgram`, the gaussian passes, `mMipChain`, `heroMipCount`) go.
6. **Quality and cost.** Oblique near plane, the glass's screen rectangle only, skip when off screen, shadow cascades of the reflected view, a render scale setting; the pink shell and thin layers checked at screen resolution (does the depth precision work at 1x?).
7. **Delete the cube path.** `mTexture` cube array of the hero manager, `updateProbeFace`, the six faces schedule, `RenderHeroProbeUpdateRate` / `RenderHeroProbeResolution` (a setting for the target scale instead), the face dump diagnostics (FACEPIX, dumps, HeroDiag, SortDiag, BridgeDiag, AlphaDiag), the Hero Radiance native manifest line.

## Risks and notes

- **Depth precision of thin layers** is the shell's suspected cause (see the near plane change). At the main resolution with the near plane at the mirror it should hold; step 6 checks it, and step 1 shows the reflected view directly.
- **Two shader families read the mirror** (native for RT off, stock for RT on) until `PLAN-rt-reflection-pass.md` removes RT from the soften variant; the lookup is one function in each.
- **Nearest mirror only**: the manager picks one hero object (as today); two mirrors in view show one reflected view. A second view per mirror is a later option.
- **The traced mirror (FSVkGlossyMirror)** is a separate path (rays) and untouched; it also stays the answer for a mirror that must show the room beyond the frustum of a planar view (the planar view sees what the real camera would see in the mirror, nothing is missing there).
- **The glass is a prim:** its box / plane come from the hero probe object (`mMirrorPosition`, `mMirrorNormal`, `heroBox`); a mirror prim must have the Dynamic flag set for avatars and particles to appear (checked 2026-11-11: the new mirror has it).
- **Not covered:** mirrors seen through other mirrors, a mirror seen from behind (the reflected camera is then on the room side: skip the view when the camera is behind the plane).
