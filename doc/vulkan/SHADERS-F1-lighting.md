# Family 1: lighting resolve and sky (note written 2026-11-08, before any new shader)

Rule (Adam): the picture stays as it is; speed is the measure; only really cheap quality gains, shown to him first. Old shaders were READ for intent, fringe cases and settings; nothing is copied.

## 1. What the family is today (RT off, the baseline of the speed work), in draw order

| step | zone / ms (crowd lap gz9) | program | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| sky haze | 0.17 | skyV/F (WL sky dome) | draws the sky into the G-buffer: per-vertex haze colour + rainbow + halo22 (moisture/ice), `x2`, clamp 5, flag SKIP_ATMOS; HDRI sky (split screen) flag HAS_HDRI |
| sky clouds | 0.12 | cloudsV/F | the cloud layer into the G-buffer, same flag |
| (stars, sun disc, moon) | small | starsV/F, sunDiscV/F, moonV/F | |
| sun program | 0.31 | sunLightV/F (+ SSAO variant) | the lightmap: sun shadow from the 4 cascades (PCF with rotation), the 2 spot-projector shadows (only when such a light exists), SSAO, into RG (+B/A spot channels) of `deferredLight` |
| soften shadow | 0.29 | blurLightV/F, 2 passes | depth-aware separable blur of the lightmap (4 taps a side, `RenderShadowGaussian/BlurSize/BlurDistFactor`), skipped with DLSS + RT shadows |
| atmospherics (the resolve) | 0.23 | softenLightV/F (41 KB fragment) | per pixel: G-buffer + depth -> position; atmospheric scattering terms (`calcAtmosphericVarsLinear`); then ONE of: PBR (probe IBL + `pbrBaseLight`), HDRI sky (copy), WL sky (`srgb_to_linear * sky_hdr_scale`), legacy material (lambert ambient + sun x min(NdotL, shadow), specular lobe via the `lightFunc` lookup, gloss env + legacy env from probes, `classic_mode` tweaks); RT reflection branches (RT track, ignored here) |
| local lights | 0.03 | pointLight/spotLight V/F (+ pull variant) | additive light volumes (boxes), projector lights with texture + shadow, fullscreen lights when the camera is inside a volume; `RenderLocalLightCount` |
| haze (x2: sky-side and water-side) | 0.06 + | softenLightV + hazeF, waterHazeV/F | the remaining atmospheric haze over the post-deferred geometry (blend ONE / SRC_ALPHA), water patches, underwater tint, water exclusion mask |

Sum for this family in the crowd lap: about 1.2 ms of the 6.4 ms GPU frame (sky 0.29 + sun 0.31 + soften shadow 0.29 + resolve 0.23 + local lights/haze 0.1).

## 2. Keep list (must not change; each needs a test spot)

1. The plaza ground shine: sun and every local light show brightly in the specular lobe of shiny legacy and PBR surfaces (resolve: `lit * scol * sunlit * spec`, `applyGlossEnv`; lights: the `lightFunc` lookup in pointLightF/spotLightF). Test: the plaza at night and by day.
2. Sun / moon switch (`sun_up_factor`), the moon as the light at night, the sun below the horizon.
3. `classic_mode` (old Windlight sky look): sunlit x1.35, `pow(NdotL, 1.2)`, the sRGB round trips, final scale 1.1. Test: a classic sky preset.
4. Material branches: legacy (sRGB albedo and specular in the G-buffer, `envIntensity`, specular alpha = gloss), glTF PBR (ORM), HDRI sky, WL sky pixels; the `baseColor.a` override of the shadow (`scol = max(shadow, albedo.a)`) and the final `mix(color, albedo, albedo.a)` (the emissive-mask/fullbright alpha of legacy materials).
5. SSAO and the ambient occlusion on the irradiance (`adjustIrradiance`, `RenderSSAOIrradianceScale/Max`), and the soft look the blur gives the cascade shadows.
6. Cascade shadows: 4 cascades, PCF with the rotation noise, the spot-projector shadows, `RenderShadowDetail` 0..3.
7. Sky: gradient from the settings, rainbow and halo22 with moisture and ice levels, cloud layer (density, scattering, shadows on the sky), HDRI skies with the split screen, stars/sun/moon, `sky_hdr_scale`.
8. Atmospherics: distance haze and fog on everything but sky pixels, water plane haze above and below water, the water exclusion mask.
9. Reflection probes: IBL, parallax boxes, probe levels, legacy env, the probe CAPTURE mode (`gCubeSnapshot`, `light_scale`, local lights darkened when ambiance is above 1).
10. Local lights: point and spot volumes, projector textures and shadows, fullscreen case, the light falloff/size conventions (`getLightFalloff`, radius x1.5 in deferred).
11. Snapshots / impostors / HUD: HUD never goes through the resolve; the probe and snapshot variants of the sun program exist (`gDeferredSunProbeProgram`).

## 3. Settings that steer the family

`RenderDeferredSSAO`, `RenderShadowDetail`, `RenderDeferredAtmospheric`, `RenderLocalLightCount`, `RenderReflectionProbeLevel`, `RenderReflectionsEnabled`, `RenderScreenSpaceReflections` (post), `RenderShadowGaussian`, `RenderShadowBlurSize`, `RenderShadowBlurDistFactor`, `RenderSSAOIrradianceScale/Max`, `RenderShadowBlurWithDLSS`, the environment (sky settings: haze, density, moisture, ice, cloud, `classic_mode`, HDRI + `sky_hdr_scale`), the water plane. Test matrix per setting: on/off or each level where a lap spot shows it.

## 4. What the new shaders need as data (the per-frame block, built in slice 1)

camera (view, projection, inverse projection, jitter), sun and moon direction in view space + `sun_up_factor` + the clamped light norm, the sky/atmosphere terms (blue horizon/density, haze horizon/density, sunlight/moonlight/ambient colours, cloud/ice/moisture), the exposure scale, render size, frame index, the cascade matrices and splits, the spot shadow matrices, the handles of the shadow maps / lightmap / G-buffer / depth / probe cube arrays, the local light list.

## 5. Where the cost can go (candidates, not decisions; each is a small program or pass change with its own A/B)

1. **Sky pixels skip the G-buffer detour.** Today the sky and clouds are written into the G-buffer (albedo + flag, `x2` and clamps) and then re-read, converted (`srgb_to_linear * sky_hdr_scale`) and copied by the resolve. The sky is at the far plane: draw it straight into the lit target before the resolve, and let the resolve leave sky pixels alone (depth test / stencil, no read). Saves the G-buffer write bandwidth for the sky area and the resolve's fetches for those pixels. Same picture if the conversion is reproduced exactly.
2. **The lightmap blur (0.29 ms) as one compute pass** with shared memory (separable, depth-aware, same taps and weights) instead of two full-screen render passes, or folded into the resolve's lightmap read. Same weights = same picture; the render pass begin/end and the two target round trips go.
3. **The sun program (0.31 ms)**: the cascade selection, the PCF taps and the SSAO are one fragment shader with many branches; split the SSAO off at the resolution it needs (check against full resolution), keep the shadow lookups; separate small programs per `RenderShadowDetail` level instead of branches.
4. **The resolve (0.23 ms, 41 KB)**: split by material branch (PBR / legacy / sky) into small programs selected by the G-buffer flag through stencil or a classification pass, so each pixel runs only its branch and the program that runs is short. Remove the dead RT-hit code from the raster path (it belongs to the RT track).
5. **Haze (x2)**: fold the sky-side haze into the resolve for opaque pixels (the pass blends over the post-deferred geometry only); keep the water patches.
6. **Local lights**: already cheap (0.03 ms) in this lap; the clustered list is for the alpha pass (family 2) and for crowds of lights.

## 6. First native programs (slice 1 order)

(a) the per-frame data block and the pass-level compare; (b) the lightmap blur (self-contained, exact spec, 0.29 ms, A/B by name swap first: `Deferred Blur Light` keeps the old uniforms); (c) the sun lightmap program split per shadow level; (d) the sky into the lit target; (e) the resolve split. After each: lap (zones, old/new windows, the plaza), Adam's look at the keep-list spots.

## 7. Results log

**7.1 Lightmap blur (native `blur_light.frag`, 2026-11-08, lap nat5, RT off, crowd spot, three old/new window pairs):** zone "soften shadow" 0.293 ms old -> 0.250 ms new (-0.043 ms, -15%), every window pair the same (0.29/0.25). The new program is 517 + 2553 SPIR-V words, compiled in 51 ms (budget 20000 words / 150 ms). Changes against the old shader (same taps, weights, rules): sky pixels return at once (nothing reads the lightmap there: the lightmap is 1.0 and the resolve takes the sky from the G-buffer), the tap positions are rebuilt with the few multiplications the projection leaves instead of the full inverse-projection multiply and divide. Not changed: 13 taps, the plane-distance rule, the weights, the sampling nudge, the sun pass-through. Picture check: Adam's eye (pending), `imgdiff.py` is too noisy with moving avatars to judge by itself. The family key stays `native_blur=0` (old) until Adam accepts; to accept the manifest gets a default-on flag.
Honest size: the pass is fetch bound (3 + 12 depth + up to 12 lightmap fetches a pixel); the sky skip and the cheaper reconstruction took 15%. A bigger step needs fewer fetches per pixel (a compute pass that fetches a tile once into shared memory, same weights): that needs compute pipelines for native programs (not built yet; the post chain will want it too).

**7.1 correction (2026-11-08):** the first blur result (0.293 -> 0.250 ms) was void: the loader copied only the first element of the kernel array, a degenerate blur that did less work. With the whole kernel copied (and arrays found by the reflection stride) the native blur costs 0.290 ms, the same as the old one: the sky skip and the cheaper tap positions bought nothing measurable (the pass is bound by its texture fetches, and the crowd view has little sky).

**7.2 Sun lightmap (native `sun_lightmap.frag`, lap nat11, three old/new window pairs):** zone "sun program" 0.293 ms old, 0.290 ms new; the same picture by Adam's eye (one blink the first time the program is switched on: the pipeline is built in the background and the draws are dropped until it is ready). 516 + 6691 SPIR-V words, 66 ms to compile. Same lookups as the old program (four cascades with the overlap blend, 5-tap PCF with the row jitter, two projector shadows, 8-tap SSAO).
**7.3 Default config (lap def1, zone timers on):** the three native programs (Copy, blur, sun) are on by default; atmospherics 0.23, sun 0.291, soften shadow 0.290, DLSS 1.31, GPU frame 6.0 ms; no device lost, no warnings. Rule: own code when the same and not slower (plan section 15).

**What the micro-rewrites taught:** reproducing a pass with cheaper position math and a sky skip does not move these passes; they are fetch bound. The gains in this family have to come from structure (fewer fetches or fewer passes): the ideas queued by Adam's go-ahead on 2026-11-08 are a half resolution lightmap with a depth-aware upsample (the blur folded into it), cheaper AO taps, the sky straight into the lit target, a compute pass with a shared memory tile.

**7.4 Half resolution lightmap (2026-11-08, laps half2 and def2; default ON, rt-debug.txt lightmap_half=0 = the old full resolution path):**
Structure: the sun lightmap is computed at half the render resolution (native `sun_lightmap.frag` with LL_HALF: each half pixel reads the full resolution pixel 2i+1 of its block), blurred at half resolution with the SAME blur program (the radius halved, two passes between two half targets), then one depth aware upsample (`lightmap_upsample.frag`: the four nearest half pixels, bilinear weights, kept only when their surface is in the tangent plane of the pixel, nearest sample as the fallback) writes the full resolution lightmap the rest of the lighting reads.
Cost (RT off, crowd spot, zone timers): sun 0.287 -> 0.127 ms, blur 0.262 -> 0.097 (at half size), upsample +0.058: lightmap total 0.562 -> 0.292 ms (-0.27 ms, -48%); the default config lap shows sun 0.12 + blur half 0.10 + upsample 0.06. Adam: shadows and ambient occlusion look as good as before (2026-11-08). Open: slight softness on MOVING avatars (thin silhouettes at half resolution; the edge fallback can take a sample of the surface behind): improve the fallback or fix up the depth discontinuities at full resolution.
Found on the way: (1) `LLRender::syncMatrices` wrote the inverse projection only `if (getUniformLocation(...))`, a bare truthiness test: a program with `inv_proj` as its FIRST uniform (location 0) never got it. Fixed (`> -1`). It had hidden behind the old programs, which never had it first. The symptom was a lightmap that was "lit" everywhere (the ambient occlusion only uses position differences and still worked). (2) A native only program (no counterpart among the old programs) is now possible: `LLShaderProgram::mNativeVertex/mNativeFragment/mNativeDefines`, used by gNativeSunHalfProgram and gNativeLightUpsampleProgram. (3) Debugging recipe that worked when logs could not tell: debug modes in the shader selected by a live key, one mode per screenshot of an automatic lap, brightness measured with Pillow (bright = true): halfdbg.ps1 style.

**7.5 Sky drawn late (2026-11-08, lap sky2; default ON, rt-debug.txt sky_late=0 = the old order):** the WL sky pool (dome haze, sun/moon, stars, clouds) was the FIRST pool of the G-buffer pass, so its haze and cloud shaders ran over the whole screen (1.1 fragments per pixel each) and the geometry then overwrote them. It is now drawn after the opaque pools of the main view (cube snapshots keep the old order): its depth test is already less-equal at the far plane, so the covered pixels are thrown away before the shader runs. Zones (windows of 25 s, RT off, crowd spot): sky haze 0.159 -> 0.017 ms, sky clouds 0.113 -> 0.013 ms (-0.24 ms); the GPU frame estimate from the DLSS share 6.43 -> 6.05 ms (coarse, +-0.1). (The time of the scene table type 0 draws moved to "pool: simple" between the old and the new order: zone attribution, not a cost.) Adam: the sky looks good, no issues at avatar edges.
Two traps found: (1) `LLDrawPoolWLSky::endDeferredPass` CLEARS THE DEPTH BUFFER (so the haze shaders can use "unwritten depth" as a sky mask). Drawn late that wiped the depth of every surface and the lighting/haze passes treated the whole screen as sky: "water layered over everything that is not fullbright". The late sky therefore writes no depth (the presets take `!sSkyLate`), does not clear, and draws the stars BEFORE the sun and moon (the moon used its depth write to clip the stars behind it). (2) A killed lap leaves the live key it had set in rt-debug.txt: check and remove it before the next lap.
Also in this change: the upsample's fallback is now the tap closest to the pixel in space (not the one with the largest bilinear weight), for the thin silhouettes of moving avatars; Adam saw no problem at avatar edges.

**7.6 Native lighting resolve (2026-11-09, `native/resolve.frag`, family key `native_resolve`, default ON (it was off for a day while its strobe was hunted; the cause is fixed, see 7.10; it was on for one commit: in the plaza of Adam's live session the whole picture strobed, the ground alternating between a dark shadowless look and the lit one, shadows also switching sides of the corner bricks, frame mean brightness 79 to 136; with native_resolve=0 the bursts were steady, 24 of 24 frames at 138, four on/off samples agreeing; BUT the resolve cannot move shadows (the sun direction changed), Adam saw the strobe take about a minute to stop although the key is read every half second, a 'cycle day N' label that changes is in nearly every screenshot (an environment cycler in his session), and two plaza laps with the native resolve on, one with the camera turning, did not strobe: the cause is NOT established and may be the environment; `native_resolve=1` turns it on; test: while it strobes flip the key 0/1 several times with a burst after each); laps rs1 / rs2 / rs3, Adam's home, RT off, zone timers on).** One program in place of the soften program's variant without ray tracing: the flag of the normal buffer picks a glTF branch (probes, screen space reflection for gloss 0.9 and up, hero probe, `pbr_base_light`), a legacy material branch (probe ambient, gloss and legacy environment, the sun's specular lobe, the same energy balance), or a sky pixel that returns right after reading its colour (the old shader worked out the sky terms for it too). New libs: `lib/poisson.glsl` (the old scatter table), `ssr_opaque` in `lib/ssr.glsl` (the old marcher with its scatter, not traced for dull surfaces, one ray for a mirror), `lib/glossenv.glsl` (moved out of material.glsl), `resolve_pass.inc`. **7.8 The strobe is the native resolve after all (2026-11-09, evening).** After the plaza checks made it default again, the strobe came back in Adam's live session ("sun freaking out", shadows switching sides). The decisive test, desktop grabs (vk-grab.ps1: CopyFromScreen, nothing sent to the viewer; PrintWindow bursts made the effect disappear) of 12 frames after each flip of `native_resolve`: on (key absent) 52% and 56% frame brightness spread, off (`native_resolve=0`) 0.3% and 0.7%, two flips each way, and Adam saw it stop and come back with them. The environment explanation was wrong. Two plaza laps with it on, one with a turning camera, did not strobe: it needs something the live session has (a frame rate held at the monitor's cap, the main thread ahead of the worker, long running probes...). Suspects, nothing proven: a texture binding the native program takes from the old program's units (the lightmap or a G-buffer target) that is stale in some frames; an environment uniform (sun_dir, sun_up_factor, lightnorm) copied by name at a moment it holds another value. The resolve is default OFF again (manifest); `native_resolve=1` turns it on for testing. Next: reproduce it in a lap (cap the frame rate with the display's refresh, no uncapped GPU bound frames) or debug in the live viewer with a launcher that points LL_VK_NATIVE_DIR at the repo (hot reload of the RESOLVE_DBG views).

**7.9 Jagged shadow edges along the avatar's arm: the half resolution lightmap's sample pixel (2026-11-09, found live with Adam's eye in the plaza, RT off, DLSS, hot reload launcher run-viewer-hot.bat).** Symptom: stair-stepped dark band along the shaded side of the right forearm and a fuzzy fringe where it meets the ground's leaf shadows; Adam had zoomed that close before the shader work and never seen it; DLAA hid it (more resolution), RR and the slim normal target did not matter. Not caught by any screenshot of mine: PrintWindow bursts (vk-burst.ps1) make the viewer repaint and the artifact goes away (use vk-grab.ps1, CopyFromScreen), and even those grabs showed nothing, so every step below is Adam's eye. Bisect: all lighting changes off together (native resolve, half lightmap, native alpha, native sun and blur, late sky): clean; back on one at a time: the half resolution lightmap brought it back. Three variants of the upsample (the program with the plane test both ways, plain bilinear, soft weights) all stayed jagged, so it is the lightmap values, not the upsample. A fixed sample at the UPPER LEFT pixel of each 2x2 block (offset 0, 0) was clean with no blink; by pixel: (0,0) clean, (0,1) clean, (1,0) jagged, (1,1) jagged (the old lower right choice): the horizontal offset decides it, the vertical one does not. Why is not understood (suspects: the x snapping of the cascade PCF, whose row jitter is a function of the row only, and the half resolution blur reading depth at the pixel edge); the fix needs no theory: the sample pixel is an explicit `half_offset` shared by sun_lightmap.frag and lightmap_upsample.frag, default (0, 0); rt-debug.txt half_fixed 0..3 picks the pixel, half_jitter=1 with the upscaler moves through the four (not needed, default off). Also learned the hard way: the full resolution path (lightmap_half=0) has the dotted dither pattern on the shadows and, in Adam's eye, a blink on camera moves or stops at certain angles and zooms; the half resolution path with x offset 0 has neither.

**7.10 The strobe's cause: a native stand-in drew with the previous draw's uniforms (2026-11-09, night).** Found with live debug views of the resolve (RESOLVE_DBG 1-15, run-viewer-hot.bat hot reload, desktop grabs, `resolve-live.ps1`): every INPUT view (pixel class, lightmap, albedo, flag, normal, specular) was steady, the normal output strobed (35-64% brightness spread), and of the lighting terms only the light direction flickered: the raw `sun_dir` took a handful of different valid unit vectors (lengths constant: not a torn copy), the sun seen from different views. In the queued draw record (`CMD_DRAW`, llvkasync.cpp) the order was: `draw_context_apply` (which swaps to a native stand-in and copies the old program's uniform values, `adoptFrom`), THEN the record's own loose uniform writes. So a native stand-in always drew with the values of the draw before: for the resolve, the previous view's sun_dir (a probe or mirror pass drawn in between, or last frame's main view). The old program uploads its values at the draw and was never affected; the packet draws (alpha) swap after the writes. Laps showed nothing because few views drew in between (the previous draw was last frame's main view, nearly the same direction); the live plaza session has more. Fix: the draw record's uniform writes are applied first, then the swap. It corrects every native stand-in drawn through this path (the sun, blur and copy programs had been one draw late all along, unseen). After the fix, with the resolve on: 24 frames at brightness 120-121 (0.1% spread), and 3 sets of grabs while Adam moved the camera showed only smooth drift (the biggest frame to frame jump 2.7). Adam's hunch that the environment loading leaves something behind in the transition was right in spirit: state left from the previous draw. The resolve's debug views stay in resolve.frag (RESOLVE_DBG, 0 = the program).
