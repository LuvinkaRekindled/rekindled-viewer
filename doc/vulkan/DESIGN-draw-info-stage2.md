# Draw info removal, stage 2: design (2026-11-12, for Adam to review before any code)

Written after stage 1 (nothing outside the producers and the fragment builders reads a draw info or a draw map any more; see `PLAN-draw-info-removal.md`, steps 3a to 6c). Everything here is from reading the code, nothing is built.

## 1. What the code does today (verified)

- **Who writes draw infos** (the only writers): `LLVolumeGeometryManager::registerFace` (`llvovolume.cpp:6068`: the merge test and the batch fields), called from `fillBatch` into a per task map (`FillTask::mMap`); the particle groups (`llvopartgroup.cpp:1601-1692`, plus the empty entries kept at 1289 and 1445); grass (`llvograss.cpp:707-774`); water exclusion and the invisible types go through `registerFace` too. The terrain paint map (`llterrainpaintmap.cpp:137`) keeps its own local vector, not a group draw map.
- **Where they go:** the task maps are merged into `group->mDrawMap` on the MAIN thread (`llvovolume.cpp:7646` for one group, `7746` for a batched rebuild), and straight after that the scene fragment is built from the group's draw map on the main thread (`llspatialpartition.cpp:518`, and `llvovolume.cpp:7755`; a batched rebuild defers the build to the batch flush with `fragmentLater`). The fragment is the only thing every view reads since stage 1.
- **What reads `group->mDrawMap` after the merge** (the full list, outside `pipeline.cpp`): `LLSpatialGroup::clearDrawMap` (bumps `mDrawMapSerial`, clears the map and the fragment: a rebuild, a release or a death), `validateDrawMap` (paranoia check, off), and the three producers above. In `pipeline.cpp`: the fragment builders (`scene_geo_fill`, `scene_fragment_fill*`, `scene_alpha_plan_fill_set`, `scene_fragment_walk_summary`, `scene_geo_attach`, `scene_fragment_ensure`, `ll_scene_fragment_build`, `scene_fragment_behind`), `shadowPrepRenderMap` (only `mDrawMap.empty()`, for a dropped counter), `renderShadow` (a counter), and the reference checks (`check_references`).
- **What keeps a draw info alive after the fragment is built** (the persistent readers, all small): `LLFace::mDrawInfo` (set in `registerFace`, grass and particles; read in `llface.cpp:500` and `llspatialpartition.cpp:4615-4623`: "is the face's new texture still the one its batch was made with, else the group must rebuild"); `GeoItem::mInfo` (`scene_geo_fill`, the plan fill: used only to make the rigged source after the loop); the fragment's own draw info pointers (`mFallback`, `mGeoFallback`, `RtExtra::mDraw`); and the builder state flags written INTO draw infos (`mGeoServed`, `mAlphaInFragment`, `mAlphaFragTex`, `mRasterFrag`, `mRigAlphaSrc`): they are read by the walk summary inside the same build, nowhere later.

So after stage 1 a draw info is only needed from `registerFace` until the end of `ll_scene_fragment_build`. Nothing later reads one.

## 2. The design: the draw map becomes transient (recommended)

**The idea.** Stage 2 in the plan says "the producers write items". Items are not a per face thing: an item's row needs the group's matrix slots, the fragment's texture numbering, the served mask over all the group's draws of a type, the plan's runs and the rigged sources, all of which are decisions over the WHOLE group, made when the fragment is built. A producer that wrote items directly would have to run the builders inside `registerFace`, which only moves them. What can be removed is the persistence: the draws exist between the producers' merge and the end of the fragment build, and are dropped right after.

- `group->mDrawMap` stays as the hand over between the producers and the builders, but is emptied when `ll_scene_fragment_build` returns (`clearDrawMap` is then only about the fragment). No view ever sees a draw info; memory drops (about 17,000 draw infos in the plaza census, and their texture lists).
- Stage 3 then deletes what is left: `group->mDrawMap` becomes a map local to the rebuild (the `FillTask` maps already are), `LLDrawInfo` is renamed into a producer local batch struct with no refcount, no builder flags, no `LLFace` back pointer; `mDrawMapSerial` becomes the fragment's own build counter; the reference checks go.

**Alternative (what the plan wording says): the producers write items directly.** Rejected for now, for the reason above: it would put the group wide decisions inside `registerFace`, whose merge test needs the previous batch of the same pass and is the most fragile code in the producers. I recommend the transient map, which reaches the same end (no persistent draw info, no mDrawMap reader outside the producers and the builder) in smaller steps. If you want the other form, step 2.4 is where it would start.

## 3. Steps (each built, lapped RT off and ray mode, and committed on its own)

- **2.0 Ownership audit (added after Adam's question, 2026-11-12), before anything else.** A draw info owns, by `LLPointer`: its vertex buffer, `mTexture`, `mTextureList`, `mSpecularMap`, `mNormalMap`, `mGLTFMaterial`, `mAvatar`, `mSkinInfo`. When the map is emptied (2.4) those references go, so for each of them something else must keep the object alive for as long as the fragment, the worker's table or a frame in flight uses it. What I know: the fragment keeps its textures as `LLPointer`s (`mGeoTex`, `RtExtra::mTex`, the rig sources, the transient items' `mVb`); the group owns its vertex buffers (`mVertexBuffer`, `mBufferMap`) and the fragment's `mBuffers` are only raw pointers with the ids they had (a staleness check, not ownership); matrices are raw pointers to the drawable's or the region's own storage (unchanged by this design). What I have NOT checked: the normal and specular maps and glTF materials of items whose entry was built from the draw info, the avatar and skin of a draw the fragment names only through an id, and the buffers' lifetime against a frame in flight (the rules of `GPU-LIFETIMES-revised.md`: its channel table says who may free what and when). The audit is a table (pointer, today's owner, owner after 2.4) and any gap is closed by an owning pointer in the fragment BEFORE 2.4. This also keeps the design inside the roadmap's order (ownership of the object layer first, then the GPU lifetimes plan): nothing here changes when a GPU resource is freed, only which CPU object holds the last reference to it.
- **2.0b Verify the premises** (no change): that nothing between the merge and the fragment build reads a draw info for a view (the batched rebuild defers the build to the flush: the group must not be drawn in between, check `fragmentLater` against `postSort`), that the async builders of `LLBuilderGuard` read no draw info any more (the shadow builders read fragments since 3n to 3o), and that release and expiry (`clearDrawMap` from the cold mode) only need the fragment. A short report, before anything is edited.
- **2.1 Builders take the draws as a parameter.** `scene_geo_fill`, `scene_fragment_fill*`, `scene_alpha_plan_fill_set`, the walk summary and the others read `const draw_map_t& draws` instead of `group->mDrawMap`; `ll_scene_fragment_build` passes `group->mDrawMap`. No behaviour change; the compare is the build and a lap.
- **2.2 The face stops pointing at its batch.** `LLFace::mDrawInfo` becomes the texture the face was batched with (`mBatchedTexture`, set where `setDrawInfo` was called); `llface.cpp:500` and `llspatialpartition.cpp:4615-4623` read that. Checked by changing a texture on a batched face in the room (the group must rebuild exactly when it did).
- **2.3 The fragment holds no draw info pointer.** `GeoItem::mInfo`, `mFallback`, `mGeoFallback`, `RtExtra::mDraw` go (the rigged source is made inside the loop); the builder state flags move from the draw info into the build's local state.
- **2.4 The map is emptied after the build** (behind a switch for one lap, then no switch): `ll_scene_fragment_build` ends with `group->mDrawMap.clear()` and counts any later reader that finds an empty map for a group with a fragment (the counter must stay 0). The groups of the batched rebuild are cleared after the flush.
- **2.5 The other producers** (grass, particle groups) already write the same map before the build: nothing to change but the empty entries kept at `llvopartgroup.cpp:1289` and `1445` (`ll_group_has_alpha_key` reads the fragment's walk bit, made from them: the bit is made from the map when the fragment is built, so they stay until stage 3).
- **2.6 Stage 3** as in the plan: the member goes, the struct is renamed and slimmed, the serial moves, the reference checks go, the plan's last sweep of switches.

## 4. Risks and what I do not know yet

- **The batched rebuild** (`LLRebuildBatch`, `fragmentLater`): between the task merge and the flush the group has draws and no fresh fragment; today it works because the group is not drawn then. 2.0 must confirm it for every view (probe faces, the HUD, snapshots) because 2.4 turns a mistake there into a missing group, not a stale one.
- **Release and wake** (cold mode, `FS_RELEASED`): `clearDrawMap` already frees the draw map and the fragment together; a woken group is rebuilt. Not read yet in detail.
- **The texture change check** (2.2) is the only reader that lives outside a view; it decides a rebuild. Getting it wrong shows as a texture that does not change, or a group that rebuilds every frame.
- **Cost:** none expected (the builders run the same work; one clear of the map after it).
- **Size:** 2.1 and 2.3 are mechanical (about 15 functions), 2.2 and 2.4 are small, 2.0 is reading. No shader or Vulkan code is touched.

## 6. Results of step 2.0 (done 2026-11-12, reading only, no code changed)

### 6.1 Ownership audit: the draw info owns nothing
`LLDrawInfo` holds handles, not counted pointers (clean core C2.3 / C2.4: the comments in `llspatialpartition.h`): `LLVBRef mVertexBuffer`, `LLTexRef mTexture / mSpecularMap / mNormalMap / mTextureList`, `LLAvatarRef mAvatar`, `LLSkinRef mSkinInfo`, `LLGLTFMatRef mGLTFMaterial`; the matrices are raw pointers. So emptying the map (2.4) drops no reference: no object can die because a draw info was cleared. Who owns what, before and after:

| What a draw info names | Draw info | Owner (unchanged by 2.4) | What the fragment keeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vertex buffer | `LLVBRef` handle | the group (`mVertexBuffer`, `mBufferMap`); freed only by the rebuild, which clears the map first | `mBuffers`: raw pointer and the ids it had (a staleness check, not ownership); `GeoRigSource::mVb`, `RtExtra::mVb` and `TransItem::mVb` are `LLPointer`s (they keep a buffer alive until that fragment is replaced) |
| diffuse, normal, specular textures | `LLTexRef` handles | the faces and their texture entries | the textures of the items, the plan and the transient items as `LLPointer`s in `mGeoTex`; `RtExtra::mTex` an `LLPointer`; the rig sources `LLTexRef` handles (resolved when used, null = skipped) |
| glTF material | `LLGLTFMatRef` handle | the texture entry | the glTF values copied into the rows (`mPbr`, `mTransPbr`); `GeoRigSource::mGltf` a handle |
| avatar, skin | `LLAvatarRef`, `LLSkinRef` handles | the avatar and the mesh asset | the same handles in the rig sources and `TransItem` |
| model and texture matrix | raw pointers | the drawable (or the region's `mRenderMatrix`) | raw pointers in `RtExtra`, `TransItem`, and the matrix slot registry (`sMovingMatrix`); a deleted drawable's matrices are handled by the rebuild rule at `llvovolume.cpp:449` (the group that names them is rebuilt), which is a rebuild of the group and so unchanged |

So the answer to "does the transient map change ownership": no. The risk is not a reference count, it is the three READERS and GUARDS below that assume the draw map is still there after the build. Two of them were missing from section 1.

### 6.2 What the draw map is still used for after the fragment build (the corrected list)
1. **`LLFace::mDrawInfo`** (known): `llface.cpp:500`, `llspatialpartition.cpp:4615-4623`. Step 2.2.
2. **`LLOctreeDirtyTexture` (`pipeline.cpp:1951`, NEW):** when a set of textures is reloaded (`dirtyPoolObjectTextures`), it walks every group's draw infos and marks the group `GEOM_DIRTY` if one uses `mTexture` in the set. After 2.4 it must ask the fragment which textures the group uses (the fragment's `mGeoTex`, `RtExtra::mTex` and the rig sources' handles cover the draws that are drawn; a draw nothing draws needs no rebuild). Added to step 2.1.
3. **The release scan (cold mode, on by default: `pipeline.cpp:2586` and `2604`, NEW):** a group is released only when `!group->mDrawMap.empty()` (it has built geometry), and the draw infos are counted for the statistic (`sRelReleasedInfos`, the scene census). With a transient map the test is always empty and NOTHING would be released any more (the object expiry would silently stop working: no error, only memory). It must ask the fragment (`mWalkNonEmpty` / `mHasDraw`, `mDrawCount`). Added to step 2.1; verified by the census line "Released: N groups" staying non-zero in a lap with the cold mode on.
4. **The builder guard (`clearDrawMap`, `llspatialpartition.cpp:219-226`, NEW and the most dangerous):** `LLBuilderGuard::beforeMutation` (it waits for the async builders in flight) is called only `if (!mDrawMap.empty())`. Since stage 1 the async builders read the FRAGMENT (rig sources, items: pointers into its vectors, valid "until the fragment is made again"), and the guard is what keeps a rebuild from freeing them under a builder. With a transient map the map is empty when the next rebuild starts, so the guard would not fire: a race (the same kind as the glTF preview crash). The condition must become `!mDrawMap.empty() || mSceneFragment` BEFORE the map is emptied (so it is the first edit of 2.4, and testable by a counter of quiesces). The epoch of the main view map (`sMainMapLive`, `sEpoch`) counted changes to the render map the early sort made; the render map is gone, the groups and fragments it names remain: check in 2.0c that the resort it triggers is still wanted.
5. **Window semantics that stay as they are** (checked, no change): `ll_scene_fragment_build` builds only `if (!group->mDrawMap.empty())`; `shadowPrepRenderMap` counts and names a group "with draws and no current fragment" by `!mDrawMap.empty()`. Both are about the window between the producers' merge and the build, where the map is exactly non-empty; after the build it is empty and the fragment is current, which is the state those tests ask for. The batched rebuild is safe for the same reason: a `LLRebuildBatch::Scope` flushes in its destructor and explicitly before the passes (`pipeline.cpp:5654`, `7965`, `9487`), and every lap of the day logged "no current fragment ... (with draws 0)": no view ever saw a group with draws and no fragment.

### 6.3 Changes to the steps
- **2.1** also converts the release scan and `LLOctreeDirtyTexture` (items 2 and 3) and the statistics that count draw infos (`sRelReleasedInfos`, `mDrawCount`).
- **2.4** starts with the guard condition (item 4), then the clear, with the counter of "a reader found an empty map for a group with a fragment" and the quiesce counter.
- **2.0c** (a small read before 2.1): the epoch mechanism of the early sort, and whether anything else is waited for by the guard.
- Nothing in 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6 changes.

### 6.4 Step 2.0c, the early sort epoch (read 2026-11-12)
`display()` makes the main view's sort early (`stateSort(..., true)`), notes `LLBuilderGuard::sEpoch`, and draws from that sort only if the epoch is unchanged; a group of the main view rebuilt in between (by the shadow preparation or an impostor) bumps the epoch through `clearDrawMap` -> `beforeMutation(in_main_map)`, and the frame sorts again (`llviewerdisplay.cpp:1096-1112`; the comment still says "freed draw infos"). The sort's result no longer holds draw infos, but it holds the fragment handles (`mGeoHandles`) and the rig sources the passes read: a group rebuilt after the early sort has a NEW fragment, so the epoch is still needed and for the same reason as the guard. It is bumped inside `beforeMutation` only when the map was non-empty: so item 4 of 6.2 covers both (the quiesce and the epoch): the condition must be `!mDrawMap.empty() || mSceneFragment`. Without it a group rebuilt after the early sort would be drawn from stale fragment handles for a frame. Step 2.0 is complete; no code was changed.

### 6.5 Correction after Adam's question about the walks (2026-11-12)
Part of the plan is to remove the walks over draw infos. Stage 1 removed the ones in the frame (the shadow render map job and its draw map loops, postSort's walk, the alpha pool's, the velocity and ray naming lists, the render map lists themselves). What 2.0 found are not per frame walks, and 6.2 / 6.3 treated them wrongly:
- **`LLOctreeDirtyTexture` is dead code, not a reader to convert.** `LLPipeline::dirtyPoolObjectTextures` (the only user of the class) has no caller anywhere in the tree. It is deleted in step 2.1, with its declaration: that removes a whole octree and draw info walk instead of re-pointing it at the fragment.
- **The release scan** (`LLPipeline::releaseScan`, from `llviewerobjectlist.cpp:1459`) is the object expiry's age scan: once every 60 frames it traverses the volume octree of every region and tests each group's age. It does not walk draw infos; its only draw map uses are the test "has built geometry" (`!mDrawMap.empty()`) and the released draw info statistic. Both become a read of the group's fragment (`mSceneFragment && mWalkNonEmpty`; the count is `mDrawCount`), no iteration. The scan itself stays: it is the expiry mechanism (an age based traversal about once a second, it was there before and is not made more expensive). Turning it into an event driven queue (a group enters a list when it was last seen, and leaves it when seen again) would remove that traversal too; that is a change of the expiry design (`EXPIRY-design.md`, the open item in the notes), not of this plan: I will not do it here unless Adam says so.
- After 2.4 the draw map is read only by the producers' merge and the builders, once per rebuild of a group: the hand over, not a walk.
