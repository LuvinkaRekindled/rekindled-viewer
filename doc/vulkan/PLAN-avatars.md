# PLAN-avatars: avatars as rows, from load to skinning (2026-11-06)

Status: planned, picked up 2026-11-06 after C5 (J2, PBR shadow rows and INVISIBLE done; BUMP / SHINY shaders, the RT material cache and J1 follow this plan). Decision: Adam, 2026-11-06 ("we might need to stop looking at avatar updates in pieces and think about how to redesign avatars completely, from load to skinning to animation"; hidden avatars may skip the pose and catch up: accepted). Work order: the clean-core plan (PLAN-clean-core.md, C5) goes on first; this plan is picked up after it.

## Why a plan and not another fix

Avatar cost has been attacked piece by piece for several sessions (the b0 skip of rigid attachments, the matrices-only update of hidden avatars, the parallel motion batch, the attachment loop as pool jobs, the far-update distance, skin cache, shadow caster LOD, rigged alpha LOD, the box override skip, the far avatar tier). Each found a few tenths of a millisecond and the cost moved to the next consumer. 2026-11-06 added three more negative or small results (below). The cost is spread over the model, not in a place: every consumer pulls its own copy of avatar state out of an object graph.

## What it costs today (RT off, a crowd of about 80 avatars, 28-31 k objects; zone laps of 2026-11-06, pool labels, attachment census)

Main thread, per frame:
- the avatar motion batch 0.80-1.2 ms: the blend wall 0.30-0.40 (29 jobs, 1.7-2.4 ms of CPU on all threads), the attachment jobs 0.06, the serial completion loop 0.49-0.54 (about 43 avatars, 11-12 us each: replay of the attachment ledger, post-motion matrices, voice, appearance, lip sync / wind, name tag, complexity every 20th frame)
- idleUpdate of the avatars 0.28, the attachment loop 0.14, the move chain of the attachments and avatars (drawable, bridge, partition moves) 0.2: about 230 bounds events a frame (bridge 113, avatar 75, animesh 23, particle 21), nearly every group of those partitions every frame
- the mark job's walked-partition part (37-160 drawables a cascade) and the walked partitions' cull in the main view and four cascades (about 0.4 ms of pool CPU)
- the worker: palettes 355 and joint matrices 10.6 k a frame (about 160 k palette floats, 0.8 MB in the ray scene naming), rigged packets (kind 21 prepare 1.1 ms of worker CPU)
- GPU: rigged triangles 10.7 M in the main view and 9.9 M in the cascades (cascade 1 more than half)

Measured and ruled out as single fixes: the skin cache across frames (4% of palettes and 12% of joint matrices unchanged), the retained set for the walked partitions (they are evented every frame), fusing the blend and attachment fork-joins (0.36 ms unfused against 0.41 ms fused: no gain, the attachment barrier is 0.06 ms), avatar update CPU as the limit in a GPU-limited crowd, back-face culling of rigged triangles (4-37% dropped).

Cuts that are quality-neutral and in: 22.9 of 36 looped avatars are skipped without the loop (98.7 attachments), static rigid attachments are not moved, the box override is skipped when unchanged, matrices-only updates for hidden avatars, alpha and opaque rigged LOD at half a pixel (looked at by Adam, default on), shadow caster LOD (2026-11-04).

Zone lap pz1 (2026-11-06, the completion loop by piece, 40.8 avatars a frame, main thread): motion batch 0.727 ms = blend 0.250 + attachment jobs 0.050 + completions 0.425; of the completions idleUpdateMisc (attachment ledger replay, world matrices) 0.190, appearance animation 0.117 (2.9 us an avatar for a function that is one flag test unless the avatar is morphing: a cache miss on the avatar object, or avatars still animating their appearance), name tag 0.092, voice visualizer 0.010, lip sync / loading / water / wind 0.007 (14.8 avatars), updateCharacterPostMotion 0.020 (27.5 avatars). Every piece is small and per avatar: the shape the avatar table is for.

## The model

One owner per fact, rows instead of an object graph, work proportional to what is seen.

1. **Avatar table.** One row per avatar: world transform, importance class (own / seen in the main view / seen only by a cascade or probe / seen by nothing), the per-view visibility stamps (from the same cull that serves the scene), distance, animation clock, flags. Name tags, voice, lip sync, wind, loading effect, complexity, impostor / jelly-doll decisions, attachment loop and the rigged naming read the row instead of asking the object. The importance class replaces the scattered isVisible() / mDrawable->isVisible() / mUpdatePeriod / far-update tests.
2. **Skeleton as flat arrays.** Per avatar: local TRS, parent index, world matrix, inverse-bind products. One batched vector pass over all avatars of the batch computes world matrices and writes the skinning palettes straight into the GPU palette buffer. No LLJoint tree walk, no palette copy into packets for each pass.
3. **Animation as data.** The motion controller (states, weights, priorities, transitions, the "walk" and "head turn" logic) stays on the main thread and only produces per-motion weights and times. Sampling the keyframes and blending is one batched job over all avatars. An avatar of class "seen by nothing" is not posed at all: it catches up from the animation clock when it is seen (keyframe motions are time functions, so the catch-up is exact; history-dependent motions (head turn, walk smoothing) settle over a few frames: accepted by Adam). Anything seen is exact: no visual cut.
4. **Attachments as joint-parented rows.** An attachment's world matrix is its joint's world matrix times its local matrix, produced by the same batched pass and written to the instance row (the instance table the ray scene and the scene table already use). No per-frame octree move, no bridge, no moved list. Culling of the movers is the GPU's, against a conservative avatar bound. Rigged attachments are skinned with the avatar's palette (already how they are drawn).
5. **Skin once per frame.** Skin each avatar mesh once into a vertex buffer that the main view, the four cascades and the ray scene read (today each pass skins in its vertex shader, the ray scene has its own skin buffer). This is the one stage whose gain is not established: the earlier "skin cache ruled out" result was about reuse across frames, not sharing within a frame (vertex runs were measured small, about 13 M a frame). Stage D starts with a measurement.
6. **Presentation as importance-gated jobs.** Name tags, voice visualizer, lip sync, wind, loading effect, complexity: per-avatar jobs run for the classes that can show them. The serial completion loop disappears as a loop of object calls.
7. **Load.** Appearance / bake / mesh fetch and decode / rigged mesh parse (bind matrices scrubbed at parse, the writer-bug fix) are priority-ordered by the importance class, and a mesh's skin data is stored in the form stage B reads (joint indices, inverse bind matrices, weights), so no per-frame conversion is left. Stage 0 inventories the load path (it is the least known part).

## Stages (each measurable on its own; census first, as always)

- **0. Census and inventory.** Per-frame cost by class (own / seen / cascade-only / hidden) of every avatar consumer, the load path timeline of an avatar from first packet to first draw, who reads which avatar field (the consumer list the table has to serve), the importance class as a logged number next to the existing tests (a mismatch counter between the classes and today's tests).
- **A. Avatar table and importance class.** The row, filled once a frame, read by the existing consumers; the old tests removed one by one behind check frames (the row against the object). Gain here is mostly structural (the completion loop starts to shrink: classes that cannot show a name tag or lip sync skip them).
- **B. Skeleton arrays and the batched pose job.** Includes the palette written to the GPU buffer, the motion controller reduced to weights and times, hidden avatars not posed. Expected: the blend wall and most of the completion loop (together about 0.9 ms of the main thread, 1.7-2.4 ms of pool CPU), the palette copies in the packets.
- **C. Attachments on the instance rows.** Removes the move chain (0.2 ms main, 230 bounds events, the walked-partition cull and mark work), the attachment loop (0.14), the moved list and the bridges. The walked partitions then hold only what is not an avatar or attachment (particles, HUD).
- **D. Skin once.** After a measurement of the shared-skin gain in the cascades and the ray scene.

## Open questions

- The own avatar and animesh (control avatars) are special in the motion controller and the loading path: stage 0 lists what they need from the table.
- Flexible-prim attachments (461.9 children a frame in the crowd) read parent positions every frame: they move with their joint, but their own physics needs a per-frame step: the row form has to keep them (stage C).
- Sitting avatars (they follow their seat), avatars on moving vehicles, and the "update root after the pose" ordering of updateCharacterPostMotion.
- RLV / attachment locks, hover offset (a bug of 2026-11-04 was a read in the middle of a deferred update), LSL animation overrides: all read the pose or the skeleton and must read it through the table's accessors.
- Where the importance class comes from for a view that has no GPU cull yet (cascades, probes): the CPU cull's stamps until the GPU cull serves them.

## Stage A design: the avatar table (2026-11-07). Written after stage 0, replaces "fix the biggest piece" as the way of working

Stage 0 is done (importance class, cost by class, pieces, motions, attachments, load: results 1-3 above, plus the consumer counts below). The own avatar's wearable write (122 us) was fixed on the way with a gate (`LL_VK_SELF_APPEARANCE`); that is the last single-piece fix: from here the pieces move into the table.

**Who decides what today (readers of the avatar's state, lines / files in newview/*.cpp):** `isSelf()` 212 / 33, `isControlAvatar()` 53 / 12, `isUIAvatar()` 22 / 2, `->isVisible()` 39 / 14, `isFullyLoaded()` 34 / 16, `isImpostor()` 30 / 6, `mNeedsImpostorUpdate` 22 / 4, `mUpdatePeriod` 21 / 3, `isCulled()` 19 / 4, `isTooSlow()` 16 / 7, `isTooComplex()` 16 / 5, `isVisuallyMuted()` 11 / 2, `shouldImpostor()` 6 / 2, far tier 6 / 2; `LLVOAvatar` is named in 104 files (pipeline.cpp 76 lines, llviewerobjectlist 17, lldrawpoolavatar 15, llvovolume 11, llspatialpartition 7, llworld 6). The update loop alone has FIVE gates that each say "does this avatar need work this frame": `idleUpdate`'s hidden-avatar period (every 2nd / 4th frame), `computeNeedsUpdate` (impostor period, far period), `updateCharacter`'s `visible` (hidden update), `farUpdatePeriod`, and the attachment loop's own skip (33.4 of 51 avatars a frame); the pipeline adds `far_avatar_tier`, `shadow_cascade_skips_avatar`, the impostor ranking (`shouldImpostor`) and the rigged LOD decisions. Each re-derives the answer from the object graph.

**The row (one per LLVOAvatar: resident, control, UI; slot kept in the avatar, freed with it).** Hot part, filled once per frame: class (own / seen / not seen; later: seen only by a probe or snapshot), distance, a rank among the visible avatars (what `shouldImpostor` computes by sorting), flags (sitting, fast, dummy / control / UI, fully loaded, impostor, too slow, muted, animesh), and the frame's MODE with its work bits: `SKIP` (impostor / far period), `MATRICES` (hidden: root follows, no pose), `POSE`; and for POSE the presentation bits NAME_TAG, VOICE, LIPSYNC, WIND, LOADING_EFFECT, COMPLEXITY, ATTACHMENT_LOOP. Cold part (later stages): offsets into the skeleton arrays and the palette buffer (stage B), the attachment rows (stage C).
**One writer, one place:** `LLAvatarTable::beginFrame()`, called once per frame before the avatar update loop. It reads the cull stamps (the previous frame's main view stamp of the avatar's drawable, as `isVisible()` does today), the object state (sit, speed, control avatar, load state) and the settings (limit non-impostors, far update distance, hidden-avatar reduction), and decides class, mode and bits. Events that change the facts between frames (appearance loaded, sit / stand, impostor switch, teleport) mark the row dirty for the next `beginFrame`. Nothing else writes the mode.
**Migration, consumer by consumer, each behind a check frame** (`LL_VK_AVTABLE` 0 old, 1 table, 2 both and count the disagreements, as the retained sets did): (1) `idleUpdate` / `computeNeedsUpdate` / `updateCharacter` take the mode from the row (the five gates become one read; `farUpdatePeriod`, the hidden-avatar period and `computeUpdatePeriod` fold into `beginFrame`); (2) the presentation pieces of `idleUpdatePostCharacter` read their bits (the name tag of an avatar nobody sees stops being updated every frame: 0.058 ms; the voice, lip sync, wind and loading effect for classes that cannot show them); (3) the attachment loop reads ATTACHMENT_LOOP (replaces its own skip tests, 33 of 51 avatars); (4) the pipeline's readers (impostor ranking, `far_avatar_tier`, `shadow_cascade_skips_avatar`, too slow / muted) read class, rank and flags from the row; (5) the rigged draw side (the render map's rigged types, palettes, rigged LOD) reads the class and the LOD decision instead of recomputing from the avatar.
**What the census says it is worth (not the reason for doing it):** about 0.1-0.15 ms of main thread (name tags and `movePartition` of unseen avatars, the five gates' tests), and the structure every later stage stands on: B writes the pose and palettes by row, C writes the attachment instances by row, the presentation jobs run by bit. The per-avatar costs that remain after A are the blend (99 us a seen avatar, 1.8 ms of pool CPU: stage B) and the attachment replay (17.8 us a seen avatar: 0.21 ms, stage C).
**Open for Adam:** where the table lives (a new `llavatartable.h/.cpp` next to `llgrouptable.h`, the same row-with-owner style), and whether `beginFrame` may read last frame's cull stamps for the class (as the code does now) or must wait for this frame's cull (it cannot: the avatar update runs before the cull; the pose it makes is what the cull and the shadow passes then use).

## Stage A step 3 read (2026-11-07): the attachment loop's avatar-level gate IS the mode; no separate bit

`idleUpdateMisc` runs the attachment loop (`replayAttachmentJob` / `idleUpdateAttachments`) only `if (detailed_update)`, and `detailed_update` is what `updateCharacter` returns: `visible` of a full update. So the avatars without the loop (33.4 of 51 a frame in the census) are exactly the ones whose row mode is NONE, SKIP or MATRICES, and the 17.6 with it are POSE and visible: the table's mode already is that gate, and a bit would only repeat it. **Step 3 as a code change disappears.** What is left inside the loop is per-ATTACHMENT logic (rigged update skipped on skip frames, `skip_static_rigid` against the last applied joint matrix, `override_bbox`, `updateMoveDampedAsync` / bridge move, `updateText`): that is stage C (attachments as joint-parented rows), not avatar-level gating. The remaining presentation pieces for unseen avatars (7.9 us each before the name tag gate: now about 4.4 us: idleUpdateMisc 3.7 of which movePartition 1.4 which has to stay (the drawable's partition follows the root), updateCharacterPostMotion 0.3) are not worth bits of their own.
**Stage A is complete as far as the update loop goes:** the five gates are one mode (default on), the name tag has its frustum fact and bit. ## Stage B design (2026-11-07): the skeleton as flat arrays, the pose and the palettes as batched passes

Written after reading the pose path in the code (llmotioncontroller.cpp, llpose.cpp, lljointstate.h, llkeyframemotion.cpp, llheadrotmotion.cpp, llskinningutil.cpp, lldrawpool.cpp `ll_frame_palette`); the numbers are the census of 2026-11-07 (plaza, 20 seen + 11 animesh full updates a frame).

### What the pose path is today, per avatar per frame
1. **Begin** (main thread, `beginUpdateMotions`): timing, loading, `purgeExcessMotions`, joint signatures. It touches the shared motion registry and asset requests: it stays on the main thread.
2. **Motions update** (`finishUpdateMotions`, a pool job in the batch): `updateAdditiveMotions`, `updateRegularMotions` -> per type, over the active motions in chronological order (**about 14 a seen avatar, 2 an animesh**): the controller works out the motion's weight from its phase (ease in / active / ease out / stopped) and calls `motion->onUpdate(time, joint_signature)`; each motion fills its own `LLPose`: one `LLJointState` per joint it touches (position, rotation, scale, usage bits, weight, priority). `mPoseBlender.addMotion` then inserts every joint state into the joint's `LLJointStateBlender` (a `std::map` pool keyed by the joint pointer: a lookup per joint state) in up to **6 slots ordered by priority**.
3. **Blend** (`blendAndApply`): per blended joint: additive states add (weighted), regular states lerp / nlerp by accumulated weight in slot order; writes the joint's LOCAL position / rotation / scale (`setPosition` etc. dirty the world matrix chain).
4. **World matrices** (`updateWorldMatrixChildren`): a recursive walk of the joint tree, `world = local x parent world`; also on demand from the 257 readers of `getWorldPosition / Rotation / Matrix` (46 files: manipulators, camera, voice, attachments, hover offset, flexi, ray scene, RLV ...).
5. **Palettes** (per (avatar, skin), `updateSkinInfoMatrixPalette` -> `LLSkinningUtil::initSkinningMatrixPalette`): for each joint of the skin: `world[j] = joint->getWorldMatrix4a()` (a pointer chase per joint) x `invBind[j]` -> `LLMatrix4a`, converted to 12 floats (`mGLMp`) in a per-(avatar, skin) cache; `ll_frame_palette` copies it into the frame's shared list under a mutex (a hash lookup per request); `ll_frame_palette_flush` sends it (kind 27): **355 palettes, 10.6 k joint matrices, about 160 k floats a frame**.
Costs (census): the blend job **99 us a seen avatar, 50 us an animesh** (1.8 ms of pool CPU a frame; the main thread waits 0.25-0.4 ms for its wall); the palette work sits in the main thread's rigged naming and in the worker's prepare.

### Two facts that make the flat form possible
- **The procedural motions read LAST frame's applied skeleton.** `LLHeadRotMotion::onUpdate` (and Eye, Targeting, WalkAdjust, PelvisFix, Editing, the physics controller: most of the non-keyframe motion classes read `getWorldPosition / getWorldRotation`) reads the joints' world transforms while the blend of THIS frame has not been applied yet (blending runs after all the motions). So the order is already: sample everything from last frame's world state -> blend once -> world pass. A flat world array from the previous frame is exactly what they read; there is no dependency on a partially blended skeleton.
- **The joint order is topological.** The avatar skeleton is loaded from avatar_skeleton.xml, parents before children, `LL_CHARACTER_MAX_ANIMATED_JOINTS = 216`: one linear pass `world[j] = local[j] x world[parent[j]]` is the whole world walk; no recursion, no dirty flags inside the pass.

### The design
**Data (per avatar, in a pool of slots like the avatar rows):** `parent[J]` (U8), local position / rotation / scale `[J]` (struct of arrays, the rotation as a quaternion), `world[J]` (3x4 rows, the form the palettes and the attachment points want), the joint remap table of the skeleton (animation joint number -> avatar joint index). Control avatars (animesh) have the skeleton of their rigged mesh: their own joint layout, the same arrays.
**What stays serial per avatar (main thread or the job, as today):** the motion controller's state machine (phase, weight, activation, stop requests, loading); the procedural motion classes (they keep their `onUpdate`, now reading `world[]` through an accessor); the emotes (visual params, no joints); the physics controller (springs on the flat world array, per avatar, serial).
**What becomes batched:** (a) **keyframe sampling**: an animation asset's joint motions become flat curve arrays (rotation keys, position keys, times) sampled for (avatar, motion, joint) tuples; (b) **the blend**: per (avatar, joint) one fold over the up-to-6 states by priority (the same math; no pool map, no refcounted joint states); (c) **the world pass**; (d) **the palette pass**: for every (avatar, skin) pair the frame needs, `out[k] = world[skinJoint[k]] x invBind[k]` written straight into the frame's palette buffer (the shared list becomes the destination: no per-(avatar, skin) cache, no copy, no mutex, no hash lookup).
**Readers of the old joint objects (257 call sites):** `LLJoint` stays a view: after the world pass its world matrix cache is written back from the array (one loop), so every reader keeps working; readers migrate to the array one by one later (the ones that matter: attachments in stage C, the camera, the ray scene).

### Steps (each behind a switch with a compare mode, verified by a lap, Adam's eye where it shows, committed; in this order)
- **B0. Flat world pass and palette pass from the EXISTING local transforms.** The arrays are filled from the `LLJoint` locals after the old blend; the world pass and the palette pass run flat; compare mode checks `world[]` against the joints' world matrices and the new palettes against the old (`mGLMp`) within 1e-5. Value: the palette copies and the per-skin cache (main thread / worker side), and the arrays everything after reads. No pose change: low risk, first.
- **B1. Flat blend.** The motions still fill their `LLPose`s; the blend reads their joint states into the per-avatar arrays (no pool map, no slots as objects) and writes the local arrays; compare mode checks the locals against the old blend. Value: the pool-map lookups and the blender objects.
- **B2. Flat keyframe sampling.** The animation assets' curves as flat arrays; the plain keyframe classes' `onUpdate` (keyframe, walk and stand are keyframe plus constraints; the constraints stay serial) replaced by the batched sampler. Compare mode checks every sampled joint state against the old one. Value: the bulk of the 99 us (14 motions x their joints x the key search).
- **B3. The motion batch becomes the pose job:** the batched passes in one job over all avatars of the frame, the serial controller states before it, the procedural motions between sampling and blend; the main thread's wait is the job's wall.
**Exactness:** the blend math is kept (lerp, nlerp, additive, 6 slots by priority); the compare tolerance is 1e-5 on matrices (SIMD changes the float order); the visible check is the usual one (avatars move, no pose pops, no stretched spikes: the palette bug of 2026-11 was exactly an uninitialised joint).
**Open / risks:** the own avatar and sitting avatars (the root follows a seat: the root joint's world comes from `updateRootPositionAndRotation` before the pass; the arrays take the root from the joint objects at the start); hover offset and ground-sit adjustments in `updateCharacterPostMotion` that write the root after the motions; attachment points and collision volumes are joints too (the arrays carry them, 216 in all); the physics motion (springs) reads and writes joint states with velocities (serial, last in the order); the pose of avatars the importance class marks unseen stays the matrices-only update (the world pass without sampling).
**What it is worth (an estimate, not a promise):** the blend job is 1.8 ms of pool CPU a frame; the keyframe sampling is data-parallel with a small working set, so B2 and B3 should take a large share of it; the main thread's wait for the pool (0.25-0.4 ms) shrinks with it; B0 takes the palette copies and the cache lookups off the main thread. Measured the same way as everything else: plaza, the crowd laps, `LL_VK_AVIMP=1` cost by class before and after.

## B3 design (2026-11-07, after reading the batch, the job and the palette path; the steps keep the plan's B3 name and place)
**What the code is today** (`run_avatar_motion_batch`, llviewerobjectlist.cpp; `LLVOAvatar::runDeferredMotionBlend`, `completeDeferredMotionUpdate`): the batch is already one pool job per avatar (`pool->runLabeled("avatar motion batch")`): `finishUpdateMotions` (about 38 us: sample, `addMotion`, blend) + the world walk (about 14 us) + the verify code when on; then the attachment jobs (A2, `LL_VK_AV_PARALLEL`); then the serial completions on the main thread (`flushDeferredStopRequests`, `updateCharacterPostMotion`, `idleUpdatePostCharacter`: a seen avatar 14.7 us = name tag 4.3, attachments 3.5, lipsync / loading / water / wind 2.1, the rest misc), with the begin part (`beginNormalMotionUpdate`: timing, loading, `purgeExcessMotions`, joint signatures) before the batch on the main thread. **So the "batch" half of B3 exists at avatar granularity. What B3 still has to change is not the shape of the job but what the job produces and who consumes it:**
- **The palettes are not in the job.** `LLVOAvatar::updateSkinInfoMatrixPalette` builds a (avatar, skin) palette lazily, on whichever thread asks first (main thread rigged naming, the pool threads of the shadow cascade packet builders, the worker), under ONE static mutex for every avatar (`palette_mutex`: it also protects `initJointNums`, which writes the skin's joint numbers, and skins are shared between avatars wearing the same mesh), then `ll_frame_palette` copies each palette into the frame list under a second global mutex with an `unordered_map` lookup per request. 355 palettes and 10.6 k joint matrices a frame, all of it serialised behind those two locks, none of it in the avatar's job where the skeleton is hot.
- **The flat world array is filled from the joints on demand** (`fs_flat_fill`: 216 `getJoint` + 216 matrix copies, once per avatar per frame at the first palette request), instead of being the output of the job's world pass.

### Steps (each behind a switch with a compare mode, a lap, Adam's eye, committed; this order)
- **B3a. Measure the palette path, then build the palettes in the avatar's job.** First a nanosecond counter in `updateSkinInfoMatrixPalette` (build time, and the time spent waiting for the mutex) so the number is known before the change. Then, at the end of `runDeferredMotionBlend` (after the world walk, the avatar's own pool job, no lock needed for the avatar's own cache): fill the flat world array once, and rebuild every palette the avatar's cache holds that was requested in the previous frame (`entry.mComputed && entry.mFrame == gFrameCount - 1`); a first request, or a skin new to the avatar, still takes the lazy path. The lazy path then finds `entry.mFrame == gFrameCount` and returns the entry. `LL_VK_PALJOB`: 0 old (lazy only), 1 job, 2 job and compare against a lazy rebuild (bit for bit, as B0). Open read before building: `LLSkinningUtil::initJointNums` (what it writes into the shared skin, and whether it can be made per-avatar or done once at the skin's load, so that a hit needs no global lock).
- **B3b. A palette request that hits takes no global lock.** With B3a most requests are hits. The static `palette_mutex` becomes a lock taken only on a miss (an atomic frame stamp on the entry says "ready"), and `ll_frame_palette` stops copying: the frame list is filled by the avatar jobs, each avatar writing its palettes at an offset it reserves with one atomic add (a request is then an offset lookup in the avatar's own entry, no `unordered_map`, no `mMutex`). - **B3d. **Order and why:** B3a is the one with a shared-state win (serialised work becomes parallel work in the job), B3b removes the locks that remain, B3c makes the array authoritative, B3d is measured main thread. Exactness: palettes bit for bit as B0 (`mode 2`), the frame list compared as a set. **Worth (an estimate to be replaced by the B3a counter):** the 10.6 k matrices a frame at roughly 30 ns is about 0.3 ms of CPU a frame today inside two global locks; in the avatar jobs it spreads over the pool and leaves the main thread and the packet builders.

## Stage C design (2026-11-07, written after reading the attachment loop, the drawable/bridge move chain and the replay; no code yet)
**What the code is today.** `LLVOAvatar::idleUpdateAttachments` (a pool job per avatar since A2, `runAttachmentJob`, with its writes to shared state recorded in a ledger of `LLPipeline::AvOp` and replayed on the main thread by `replayAttachmentJob`) walks every attachment of the avatar and, per ATTACHED OBJECT: (1) `gPipeline.updateMoveDampedAsync(drawable)` = `LLDrawable::updateMoveDamped`: `updateXform` (the object's world matrix from its parent, the attachment point's `LLXformMatrix`), `moveUpdatePipeline`, `updateText`; (2) for a rigid attachment `gPipeline.updateMoveNormalAsync(bridge)`: the same for the linkset's `LLAvatarBridge` drawable (the bridge's xform, then its spatial extents from its own octree root bounds transformed by that matrix, then `movePartition` = a move in the region's avatar partition octree); (3) for a rigged one `override_bbox` (the bridge's extents are set to the AVATAR's box) and `bridge->updateMove()` + the group's draw order. The writes (moved list, moved bridge, partition move, bridge move, render order, make active shared, update text, dirty group) are the ops the replay makes on the main thread: **0.65-0.75 ms a frame and 37-38 us per avatar at the safe hub (17-20 seen avatars), 17.8 us at the plaza**, the largest piece of the main thread's avatar frame. The draws read the bridge's xform world matrix through `LLDrawInfo::mModelMatrix` (a pointer; the scene table and the ray scene take it as a "moving" matrix slot, `scene_moving_acquire`, and read it every frame).
**Two cuts already in and kept:** the static rigged attachments are updated every 8th frame (staggered), the rigid ones whose joint matrix is within the tolerance of the one the last move used (`mAttJointApplied`) skip the drawable and bridge moves (`FSSkipStaticRigidAttachmentUpdates`). At the plaza 37 of 91 rigid attachments a frame skipped; 57-60 moved more than 0.1 mm.
**The model (item 4 of the model above), made concrete.** An attachment follows a joint: world = joint world x the object's local matrix; nothing else about its transform changes while it is attached and plain (`att_plain`: not selected, flexible, animated, lit, sounding, particle emitting, HUD). So for a PLAIN attachment the whole per-frame chain above reduces to one matrix product and one store, and the octree move is unnecessary if the bridge's extents are the avatar's box (conservative: exactly what a rigged attachment already uses).
