# Avatar self shadows (hair on the face, a hand over the shoulder): what was tried, 2026-10-27 / 28

Goal (Adam): hair and attachments cast lighter, softer sun shadows on the avatar; people take screenshots of their avatars, so small details matter. Fable's plan for this is `PLAN-rt-hair-shadows.md`; this note is what the tests found. Everything below is off by default (the defaults look as before) except the three shipped changes marked **default**.

## Shipped as defaults (committed 391dfc33)
- **Soft alpha ray** (`rtShadowRaySoftS`) for the sun up to 12 m of view distance: a masked or blended card blocks the fraction its (blurred, `lod + 2`) alpha covers, not all or nothing. Continuous hair shadows. `LL_VK_RT_SUN_SOFTALPHA=0` turns it off.
- **Ray start offset** along the normal 0.003 m (was 0.015): the 1.5 cm dead zone cut a strand's shadow off where it came close to the face. 0.015 stays for blended receivers (`LL_VK_RT_SUN_BIAS_ALPHA`): hair cards a few mm apart shaded each other grey when it was small.
- Minimum distance along the ray (`LL_VK_RT_SUN_TMIN`) is off (0): it was the first answer to hard streaks (0.04), not needed now.

## Findings
- **Nothing tells the viewer a draw is hair.** The attachment point is arbitrary for rigged mesh (Adam's hair is worn on a hand). The skin info's joint list does not separate hair from a head mesh: both list `mHead mNeck mChest mTorso mPelvis mFaceRoot RIGHT_PEC LEFT_PEC` (skins list the whole ancestor chain). Per-vertex weights would not either (both follow the head). Only the attachment's inventory name (own avatar) or point (head points) are signals. BoM bakes mark skin, not hair cards.
- **Hair shadows come from both groups in the ray scene:** solid hits (opaque skinned draws: the hair's base) and the alpha loop (cards). The strength cap as first written (per card) did nothing: a lock is dozens of cards and the product stacks; it is now a floor on the total.
- **Debug views** (`LL_VK_RT_SUN_DEBUG` 1 = only solid hits, 2 = only the alpha loop): mode 1 shows holes in an avatar's ground shadow by design (masked parts are dropped); mode 2 shows the hair cards as scattered triangles. Not a bug. The candidate limit (`LL_VK_RT_CANDIDATES`, `LL_VK_RT_CAPDEBUG`) was ruled out.
- **Distance rule** (`LL_VK_RT_SUN_ALPHA_STRENGTH` = the most light the close skinned avatar surfaces together may block, `LL_VK_RT_SUN_SELFNEAR` = how close, `LL_VK_RT_SUN_PENUMBRA` = width of the blur of a close self shadow): needs no hair detection. A skinned avatar surface within SELFNEAR of the pixel it shadows is translucent; farther away, and non-avatar geometry, blocks fully (the ground shadow stays dark). Solid skinned records carry `kRecSkinned` for it (`llvkrt.cpp`, one line). At strength 0.6, near 0.35: hair shadows on the face and chest much lighter, ground shadow unchanged (Adam: "getting closer").
- **Problems left:** (1) a hard distance cutoff doubled the hand's shadow (light fingers inside the limit, dark wrist outside): the floor now fades between 40 % of SELFNEAR and SELFNEAR. (2) The shadow looks like a projection: the lips are readable in the chin shadow, a crisp low-contrast copy, because skin scatters light a few mm and a ray test does not: `LL_VK_RT_SUN_PENUMBRA` (a wider disc for the other rays of a pixel whose first ray met a close skinned surface, radius penumbra / t) was written for it, **left off** (Adam, 2026-10-28), not judged yet. (3) **X-ray:** fingers behind a hand add to the shadow through the hand: the fade uses the FARTHEST close skinned hit (`t_skin`, max), so a ray through palm and finger fades to a darker floor than one through the palm alone. It should use the nearest hit (`rt_near_skin_t`, min): the first occluder decides, what is behind it adds nothing (a silhouette, not a projection of the inside).

## Update, end of the session (2026-10-28 01:10): where it stands
Tried after the first commit, all off by default (`LL_VK_RT_SUN_...` knobs): the fade of the floor now uses the NEAREST close hit (fixed the fingers showing through a hand: Adam, "that helped with the fingers"); `PENUMBRA_RAYS` (default 8: a pixel whose first ray met a close skinned surface takes more rays on the wide disc); `SELFFADE` (fraction of SELFNEAR where the fade starts, 1 = no fade; `rtSelfFloorScale` handles that case).

**Not solved, and why (read before trying again):**
- The shadow of the lips and the head under the chin is still a **projection** (a stack of contours: a lighter outer region, a darker disc, two horizontal lines) in a snapshot. Removing the distance fade (`SELFFADE=1`, near 1.2 m) did not change it (Adam, 01:10). So the contours are not (only) the fade.
- The wide disc only widens a pixel whose FIRST ray hit a close surface, so the blur stops at the silhouette edge and the outer edge stays crisp. A symmetric answer needs something that does not depend on the first ray: a screen-space bilateral blur of the sun lightmap on avatar pixels (the plan's option C: avatar mask = alpha of the avatar velocity map, depth aware, a few pixels), or a probe ray with an instance mask for avatars.
- A snapshot has no shadow accumulation and 2 rays per pixel (`accumulateSunShadow` returns early for `gSnapshot`); more rays or a blur is the only smoothing there. The live view accumulates 6-8 frames.
- The geometry of the shadow is physically right (the sun does shine past the lips onto the neck); what is wrong is what it looks like: a real face scatters light a few millimetres under the skin. That is why a ray test alone looks like a projection.
- Things that were wrong guesses, to not repeat: hair identified from the skin's joint list (it cannot tell hair from a mesh head), the candidate limit, the scene membership (the avatar's draws are steady), soft-alpha strength as a per-card factor.

## 2026-10-28, second session: two causes found by reading, not yet judged

1. **The contours of the chin shadow were the head's inner shells.** `rtShadowRaySoftS` walked past a close skinned solid hit up to three times and returned fully dark on the fourth. A mesh head is several closed shells (face, mouth bag, two rows of teeth, tongue, eyes): a ray through the face alone crosses two (floor), one through the mouth crosses more than four (dark). Lighter outer region, darker disc, two lines: the mouth and the teeth. That is why `SELFFADE=1` changed nothing. Now the ray stops walking the avatar after the first close solid hit and casts one world-only ray behind it (a tree over the avatar still blocks). For that, avatar instances have their own mask bits (4, and 8 with masked geometry; the world keeps 1 and 2) and every ray traces with `0x0F`; debug mode 4 (`0x04`) still finds every avatar. No knob: the walk through the shells was wrong.
2. **Blended hair was shadowing as a cut-out.** A `KIND_ALPHA` entry carries cutoff 0.598 (the shadow maps' cut for blended surfaces) and `rtAlphaCover` ramped `(alpha - 0.598) * 3 + 0.5`: a wisp under alpha 0.43 cast nothing, anything over 0.76 blocked like a solid. A blended card now covers by its alpha (record flag `ALPHA_BLENDED` 512, set in `add_entry` for `KIND_ALPHA`). `LL_VK_RT_SUN_BLENDCOVER=0` brings the ramp back for an A/B.

**The probe was reverted (Adam, 02:20) and put back (02:35).** Adam saw the hair shadows on the face lose their softness in the run with the probe; the revert to the build without it did not bring the softness back ("apparently we won't get it back to the state it was in"), so the probe was not the cause. What else changed between 01:31 and the later runs: the sky was switched to midday at 02:03 for a sanity check (a near-vertical sun makes a lock's shadow short and hard; the 01:31 snapshot has a low sun and a long ground shadow); not confirmed. Adam's decision: keep the probe, and add a knob for softer shadows.

**`LL_VK_RT_SUN_SSS`** (metres, **default 0.004**, 0 = off, launcher argument 5): each ray of a pixel starts at a random point of a disc of that radius on the receiver's surface (skin scattering): the edge softens on both sides of a silhouette, which the sun-disc penumbra could not do. No extra rays; the accumulation averages the start noise. Adam at 4 mm (02:45): "that worked", made the default. Not yet: a cost check, and the accumulation's 3x3 clamp may eat part of it on avatar pixels (widen the margin for `vel.a` pixels if so).

## Defaults as of 2026-10-28 02:50 (the state of `run-viewer-chin.bat` with no arguments, Adam: "why would I want it in any other state")
`LL_RT_SUN_ALPHA_STRENGTH` 0.6, `LL_RT_SUN_SELF_NEAR` 0.8 (fade from 0.32), `LL_RT_SUN_SELF_RECV` 0.02, `LL_RT_SUN_SSS` 0.004, `LL_RT_SUN_BLEND_COVER` 1, soft alpha 12 m, start offset 0.003 / 0.015. Off: `PENUMBRA`, `HAIRFILL`, `TMIN`. A normal launch now has the lighter self shadows, the soft edges, the blended hair cover, the chin walk and the full ground shadow at the feet.

Adam, 01:31: "that worked great" for the chin and the hair. His snapshot showed the cost: **the ground shadow faded toward the feet.** The distance rule only looked at the caster: a ground pixel at the feet is within SELFNEAR of the legs, so the legs got the floor. The 4-shell walk had hidden it (feet plus shoes are more than four shells: dark by accident). The rule needs its other half, the receiver must be an avatar too, and nothing in the lightmap pass says what a pixel is (the G-buffer flag channel is a four-level float, full; the avatar velocity map exists only with the upscaler). The ray scene says it: `rtReceiverIsAvatar`, a probe from 2 cm under the receiver to 2 cm over it along its normal into the avatar structures alone (mask `0x0C`, `LL_VK_RT_SUN_SELFRECV`, default 0.02, 0 = off). Only a pixel whose first ray met a close avatar surface pays it, and a non-avatar receiver traces that first ray again at strength 1. Adam: the ground at the feet is full again, "looks good".

**Verifying the probe (02:13):** Adam thought the hair shadows on the face were a little harder with the probe than without. Three debug runs that drew the probe's answer as a brightness (lit = avatar) were unreadable: a frame with no cast shadows looks the same whether the sun term is 0 or 1, by eye and by measuring the picture. What settled it: a live instance dump (`instdump=1` in `<work>\rt-debug.txt`, read every 0.5 s while the viewer runs, written to `<work>\rt-instances.log`): 5506 instances of mask 1, 2864 of mask 2, one of mask 12 (the avatar); and `LL_VK_RT_SUN_DEBUG=3` redrawn as **stripes** (horizontal where the 4 cm probe misses, vertical where a 50 cm one misses): the ground and walls crosshatched, the avatar clean, hair included. The probe is right; an avatar receiver gets the identical floor with or without it, and no mechanism for harder hair was found. Rule for debug views from now on: patterns, never a brightness, and say beforehand what each outcome looks like.
