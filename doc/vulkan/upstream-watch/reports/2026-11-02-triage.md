## (c) Aurora ideas (GPL-3.0: ideas and measurements only, never code)

Of the 48 new commits, 16 were flagged; 7 render/perf ones read (commit messages, not code), plus the 4 from the 2026-11-01 skim that the log said go into the first real triage. Grouped by what they are for us:

**Worth a plan entry**

- **`8dae573ec` (#1802) texture memory lowers once and holds after a teleport.** *Idea plus measurement.* Touches our `LLViewerTexture::updateClass` bias loop (`llviewertexture.cpp` ~570-640, the `VkBudget` log lines). Their three faults: (1) a step lowered every texture finer than its screen size at once (4.9 GB -> 0.85 GB for 55 MB over target), (2) the driver's `VK_EXT_memory_budget` figure follows the viewer's own usage, so the target swung 2.7 to 4.9 GB and the loop chased itself, (3) the step back counted nothing that would load back. Their fix: lower the largest textures only until 85 % of target, give out the lowest budget of the last 60 s (a drop counts at once, a rise after the whole window), and count the reload estimate from step 1. First thing for us: read a `VkBudget` log after a teleport into a busy place and see whether our bias oscillates. Candidate entry 7 (or under 2, the BC7/cache item, since it decides the budget pressure).
- **`31457b684` (#1806) background texture reads overflowed their thread's stack.** *Pattern, not a feature.* A pool thread waiting inside a parallel loop ran other queued batches on the same stack, nesting without bound (238 KB of nesting in a 200-batch test, a crash at 116k objects). Our fork-join pool had its own double-claim bug (fixed 762537ad); the question is whether a waiting worker on our side helps with other jobs on the same stack, and how deep that goes under a texture flood. A cheap check with the standalone stress test, one line in the plan's "smaller ones" section.
- **`6e19a4f53` (#1929) Intel Iris Xe: texture pages held to `maxUpdateAfterBindDescriptorsInAllPools`.** *Check for the low-spec matrix.* Intel's Windows driver allows 1,048,576 descriptors across all update-after-bind pools; 64 sets x (16384 + 2) overflowed it by 128 and the device was lost as out of memory. Our `llvkcontext.cpp:819` sizes the bindless pages from `maxDescriptorSetUpdateAfterBindSampledImages` only; we never read the all-pools total. One-line check when the deferred low-spec matrix runs (Intel has not been tried on our side).
- **`aa7573c2a` (#1842) 4K shading measurements and dead ends.** *Measurement only.* On their objects pass at 3840x2054: sun shadow 2.7 ms, local lights 1.1 ms, atmospherics 0.6 ms, geometry about 6 %; a 16-sample shadow filter saved nothing, a shadow-in-its-own-compute-pass saved nothing overall, a classic-sky branch saved nothing. Layered transparent hair: four rigged-blend passes are half the transparent cost; one pass is 1.9 vs 3.8 ms at 4K. Relevant to our GPU-bound state (12.5 of 12.8 ms at Adam's yaw) and our own hair pass work: it says where not to look (filter sample count, a separate shadow pass) and that the sun shadow term is the biggest single shading cost on a similar design. Note under the GPU-bound item, no build.

**Already done on our side, or not ours**
